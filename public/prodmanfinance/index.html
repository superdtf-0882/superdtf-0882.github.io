Finance for Product Managers

Product managers need to master internal economics just as well as market economics. Every project requires capital, which has a price. Your project or initiative needs to earn more than that price. Here's how to think about it in terms your CFO will appreciate. The numbers below are an example; replace them with yours.

What shareholders expect from your company

Shareholders could put their money in an index fund instead. The cost of equity is the return they expect from your company to make its extra risk worth taking. Finance estimates it with a formula called CAPM: a safe return, plus a reward for risk, scaled by how much your company's stock swings.

%

The yield on 10-year US Treasury bonds: what an investor earns with essentially no risk. Finance teams call it the risk-free rate. Look up today's yield.

×

Called beta. 1.0 moves with the market; 1.5 rises and falls 50% more than the market. Private company? Finance uses similar public companies. Software firms often sit between 1.1 and 1.4.

%

The long-run yearly return expected from the market as a whole, such as the S&P 500. Most analysts use 8 to 10%.

The bar your project has to clear

Your company pays for everything with a mix of borrowed money and shareholders' money. The blended cost of the two is the weighted average cost of capital, or WACC. A project that earns less than this is spending money that costs more than it makes.

WACC is the bar for a project as risky as the business already is. A new product or market is usually riskier, so it has to clear a higher bar.
